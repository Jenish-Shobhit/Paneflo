Every window.
In its place.

Paneflo is a menu bar app for your Mac that finds, arranges and switches between individual windows: a ⌘Tab that lists windows instead of apps, keyboard tiling, and an organized Overview that stays where you put things.

Download Paneflo Install steps

For macOS 26 or later on Apple silicon. Free and open source (MIT). Not notarized yet; the install steps cover the one-time Open Anyway.

What it does

Three modules, one list of your windows

Turn on the ones you want. They share a single window list, so what you see in the switcher is what you can tile and organize.

A switcher for windows, not just apps

Hold ⌘ and press Tab (Shift goes back) to pick a single window. Arrow keys choose too, Esc cancels, and releasing ⌘ brings the window forward.

With previews on, the selected window shows a live picture and the others show recent snapshots. A small symbol marks a minimized, hidden or paused window, and VoiceOver says whether a picture is live or a snapshot.

Previews are optional and need Screen Recording

Tiling, and tiled pairs

Hold ⌘⌥ in the picker, or ⌃⌥ anywhere, and press an arrow: left or right for a half, up to fill the display, down to undo. ⌃⌥Z undoes the last move.

Two windows in opposite halves of one display become a pair, shown side by side with a link in ⌘Tab. Opening one brings its partner forward, or open it alone. Snap assist outlines the other half so you can choose the partner.

An organized Overview

Press ⌃⌥Space, or swipe three fingers up on the trackpad, for a panel of every window in slots you control. Arrows move spatially, search keeps slots in place, and Return focuses.

A slot freed by a closed window waits for the next new one. Drag a window onto a free slot to place it, or onto another window to swap.

Gesture is optional and experimental
three-finger swipe up

The desktop, in the loop

A Desktop card ends the ⌘Tab loop and a Desktop tile sits in the Overview. Choose it to reveal the desktop by hiding other apps; choose it again, or any window, to bring things back. You can turn it off in Settings.

Settings, Shortcuts & Help

Settings turn each module and option on or off: the selected window's title, card size, tile pairs, snap assist, free slots, the gesture, and updates. Shortcuts & Help lists every key, and the menu bar menu has Switch Windows…, Arrange Windows…, Overview and a status line that says whether ⌘Tab is ready.

⌘Tab⌃⌥Space

Paneflo's own overview. It is a panel Paneflo draws; it does not change Apple's Mission Control or Spaces. The trackpad gesture is experimental: it uses an unsupported macOS interface, needs Apple silicon, and checks at runtime that the trackpad data looks as expected, so Settings tells you when it is unavailable. The system three-finger Mission Control and App Exposé swipes must be turned off first.

Install

About a minute, plus the permissions macOS asks for

Paneflo is a plain download: no installer, no account.

  1. Download the zip from the button above and open it. You get Paneflo.app.
  2. Move Paneflo.app to your Applications folder and keep it there: macOS ties Paneflo's permissions to that copy, and updates replace that copy.
  3. Open Paneflo. It isn't notarized by Apple yet, so macOS blocks the first launch once:
    1. In the "Paneflo" Not Opened dialog, click Done.
    2. Open System Settings › Privacy & Security and scroll to Security.
    3. Next to "Paneflo" was blocked, click Open Anyway, then confirm with your password or Touch ID.
  4. Set up Paneflo opens. On Give Paneflo access click Grant… on each row, switch Paneflo on in the System Settings pane that opens, then come back: the row turns green by itself. Accessibility and Input Monitoring are required; Screen Recording is optional (window pictures).
  5. If a row says Restart Paneflo to apply, click Restart Paneflo. Otherwise click Continue, skip or set up the gesture, and finish. ⌘Tab works as soon as the required permissions are on.
  6. For the optional gesture: in System Settings › Trackpad › More Gestures, turn off the three-finger Mission Control and App Exposé swipes (or move them to four fingers). Paneflo doesn't change them for you. Then turn the gesture on in the setup window's gesture step, or in Settings › Overview & Gesture.

Permissions and requirements

What it asks for

macOS shows each permission only when you click Grant, and Paneflo explains what it is for first.

Accessibility
To find other apps' windows, focus them and move or resize them. Without it Paneflo can't see or place anything. Privacy & Security › Accessibility.
Input Monitoring
To catch ⌘Tab, the tiling shortcuts and the trackpad gesture. Privacy & Security › Input Monitoring.
Screen Recording (optional)
Only if you turn on previews, for window thumbnails and the one live preview. Without it the switcher still works, with no pictures. Privacy & Security › Screen & System Audio Recording.

Requirements

  • macOS 26 or later on a Mac with Apple silicon.
  • Free and open source under the MIT license. No account, no analytics.
  • See the implementation status for what has and hasn't been verified and its known limits (other Spaces and full-screen windows, for one).

Updates

Updates itself from GitHub, only when you choose

Updates are optional, checked, and kept to your signing identity.

  • Choose Check for Updates… in Paneflo's menu bar menu. If a newer version exists you see its release notes and can Install and Relaunch, choose Later, or Skip This Version.
  • Paneflo also checks once a day, quietly, and never while the switcher or Overview is open. Turn that off with Check for updates automatically in Settings › Updates; with it off, nothing is requested until you ask.
  • Verified before it's installed. Paneflo checks the download's size and SHA-256, its bundle ID and version, and that its code signature is valid and made with the same certificate as the copy you're running. Anything else is refused.
  • The old copy goes to the Trash, the new one takes its place and opens, and your permissions carry over because the signing certificate is the same.
  • A copy built locally without the "Paneflo Dev" certificate can't verify a download; install from the releases page instead.

Privacy

Nothing leaves your Mac except an update check

Paneflo has no analytics, no telemetry and no accounts. Its only network traffic is asking GitHub for the latest release, and downloading it when you choose to update. Nothing about you, your Mac or your windows is sent.

FAQ

Questions

macOS says Paneflo can't be opened. Is it safe?

Paneflo isn't notarized by Apple yet, so Gatekeeper blocks the first launch. Open System Settings › Privacy & Security, find the message about Paneflo near the bottom, click Open Anyway and confirm. You only do this once. The source is public, and the signature of every update is checked against your installed copy.

I turned the permissions on but a row is still off.

macOS can keep a permission entry for an older build. In Set up Paneflo, click Permissions stuck? Reset and grant again. It clears Paneflo's own entries only and walks you through granting them again. If a row says Restart Paneflo to apply, restart it.

The three-finger swipe does nothing, or opens Mission Control.

Turn off the system's three-finger Mission Control and App Exposé swipes first (System Settings › Trackpad › More Gestures), then enable the gesture in Settings › Overview & Gesture. The gesture is experimental, and Settings says when it is unavailable on your Mac.

I hid the menu bar icon. How do I get Paneflo's menu back?

Open Paneflo again from Spotlight or the Applications folder. That brings back the icon and its menu.

Does it replace Mission Control or Spaces?

No. The Overview is Paneflo's own panel. It doesn't change Apple's Mission Control or control native Spaces.

Why does it need Input Monitoring?

To notice ⌘Tab, the tiling shortcuts and the trackpad gesture while another app is in front. Paneflo does not record or store what you type.
